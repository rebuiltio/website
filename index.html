Your week, in your words.

“Open enrollment is seventy-hour weeks doing the same lookups as last year.”

You hired good people to grow an agency. They spend their days as human APIs between systems that don't talk to each other. Data moves from one portal to another. A change in one place gets re-entered in four others. Over and over, every day, forever.

Your back office caps your growth, and it is one resignation away from stopping.

What I'll do

  • Take the minutia off every desk I work alongside, so each person on it does twice the work.
  • Keep every open case sorted, send the follow-ups, and chase what's late.
  • Tell you the one thing you need to decide, with what I know about it.
  • Narrate my own progress, and ask for help when I hit a wall.
  • Change how I work when you tell me the process changed.

What I won't do

  • Sell, or anything that needs a license. That stays with your agents. I do the minutia so they have time to sell.
  • Make the crucial calls. I prepare them. You decide.
  • Work outside your rules.

When the next person leaves, the process stays. The new hire learns it from me.

What happened at our first customer.

Their contracting coordinator gave notice. She ran that desk alone. The owner asked who was going to train her replacement.

Her process already lived in a digital employee. So he trained the replacement.

She does twice the work she did before. Nobody was fired. She kept the judgment.
